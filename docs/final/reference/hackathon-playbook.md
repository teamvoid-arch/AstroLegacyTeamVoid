# Astro Legacy — Research Brief & 48-Hour Hackathon Playbook

Challenge: **Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars** (2026, Intermediate / Beginner-Youth). Target audience: the challenge says **school-age space enthusiasts**, so design first for ages 8–15 (older fans still enjoy it). Research date: October 9, 2026.

---

## 1. The rule that matters most

The official Participant FAQ says: *"Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon."* The Terms & Conditions describe submitted content as *"content that you have created for the NASA Space Apps Challenge"*.

**What this means for us:** the current Astro Legacy app (built in October) is a **practice prototype**. Use it to learn, test ideas and train the team. During the hackathon (Nov 14–15), build the submission fresh, using this playbook. If you want to reuse anything from the prototype, ask your Local Lead first and get the answer in writing.

Research, studying past winners, learning tools and planning are normal preparation.

## 3. How judging works

**Local → Global Nominees → Global Finalists → Global Winners.**
1. Local Event judges choose Global Nominees.
2. NASA and Space Agency Partner experts choose Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions.
3. An executive committee chooses 10 Global Winners.

Every stage uses the same **five criteria**, weighted equally:

| Criterion | Judges ask | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Influence** | How big is the problem, how many people helped? | Free, mobile-first, no sign-up; English + Bangla; ages 10–30 |
| **Creativity** | Is it new? | Machines narrate their own lives; a teen guide; activities that replay real NASA numbers |
| **Validity** | Scientifically sound, works for real? | Every caption sourced; uncertainties shown; calculations labelled |
| **Relevance** | Answers the challenge, complete, usable? | Moon + Mars + deep space; hardware + science + what happened next |
| **Presentation** | Is the story of the project told well? | The product is a story; strong 30-second demo |

Target award: **Best Use of Storytelling** — "the project that most creatively communicates the potential of open data through the art of storytelling". Also eligible: Most Inspirational, Art & Technology, Best Use of Data, Local Impact.

## 5. Story design for ages 10–30

- **Two voices.** The machines speak in the first person, using facts only. A fictional teen guide (Nadia, 15, Bangladesh) gives the human thread: a blackout, a question from her little brother, a search, and an answer.
- **Five acts plus prologue.** Prologue (the question), I Moon, II Mars, III Wings over Mars, IV Still Traveling, V Not Forgotten.
- **One idea per screen.** About 20–35 words per caption.
- **Agency.** Readers choose where to go first; six "Try it" activities; a sorting game that tests the core idea (old ≠ abandoned).
- **Tone and look.** Bright and friendly, not babyish: a colourful night sky, chunky labelled buttons, one colour and icon per chapter, rounded fonts that work in English and Bangla, plain words ("Chapter 1", not "Act I").
- **Trust.** A small astronaut beside every caption opens a mission file with the NASA source; "What we don't know" notes.
- **Opening.** Minimal text: a 3D solar system, the title and one button. *Begin the story* starts a designed cinematic sound and a female storytelling narrator; scrolling flies to the real landing sites on the Moon and Mars. Mute buttons stay in the top bar.
- **Kid-safe media.** Check every image for suitability for 10-year-olds (we replaced the Pioneer plaque photo with our own clothed pictogram drawing).

## 6. 48-hour build plan

**Before Nov 14 (allowed prep):** read the Challenge Statement (Oct 28); join Space Apps Connect; agree roles; set up accounts (GitHub, Netlify); practise the tools on the prototype; collect the NASA source list (SOURCES.md).

**Roles (up to 6):** Story lead · Research/fact-checker · Illustrator · Frontend dev ×2 · Video & submission lead.

| Hours | Goal |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Confirm challenge details; lock the 9 machines and the 6 activities; write a one-line arc |
| 3–10 | Script all captions (EN), each with its source link; fact-checker signs off |
| 3–16 | Illustrations (own art or SVG); start the app: splash, scroll stage, captions |
| 10–20 | Bangla translation by a native speaker |
| 16–30 | Activities, info cards, ending (status cards, quiz, certificate) |
| 30–36 | Test on phones; accessibility pass; deploy to Netlify |
| 36–42 | Record a demo video (≈ 30 s hook + walkthrough) with English subtitles |
| 42–48 | Fill in the submission: Summary, Demo, Project link, Details, AI use, NASA data + References; submit early |
