# Cards and Mini-games

Every machine that doesn't get a video gets a card. A card is not a fact sheet: it teaches **one idea** with a picture, two short lines and, often, a 20-second mini-game. A kid should finish a card knowing *why* that machine mattered, not just *what* it was.

## The card

| Part | What goes there | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture | A real NASA photo with a slow push-in, a short real NASA video or animation, or a 3–5 s painted loop (Google Flow) | Never an empty card. A real photo gets a small "NASA photo" tag |
| Title chip | Name + year + place (Moon / Mars / Deep space colour) | One line |
| Big line | What it did, in kid words | 10 words or fewer |
| Why it matters | What it taught us, or how it connects to today | 20 words or fewer |
| Mini-game | Do it yourself: one tap, drag, hold or slider | 20 seconds or less |
| Status sticker | Still working · Resting · Silent · Crashed on purpose · Lost | Same colours as the stamps |
| Source | One small "NASA source ↗" link | One link only |

**Flip side (optional).** Tap the card to flip it: VIMO's one-line reaction and the machine's own voice line. Nothing else.

## How it should look and feel

- **Painted, not plain.** The card is a thick-outlined sticker card on the painted world: rounded corners, soft shadow, a coloured edge glow (Moon blue, Mars orange, Deep space violet).
- **The picture is 60% of the card.** Text sits on a soft panel at the bottom, never on top of the photo's subject.
- **Big type, few words.** Baloo 2 for the big line (28 px), Nunito for the rest (18 px). No paragraphs. No lists.
- **It arrives with life.** The card pops in with a little bounce and a "whoosh", the photo slowly pushes in, the status sticker slaps on with a "thup".
- **VIMO is on every card.** Peeking from the corner, reacting to what the kid does (gasp, giggle, thumbs-up).
- **Done = stamp.** Finishing the card or the game thumps a stamp into the logbook with a "ka-chunk".

## Mini-game rules: learn by doing

Each mini-game follows **Do → See → Learn**:

1. **Do:** the kid does one simple action (tap, drag, hold, slide).
2. **See:** something happens right away, with sound.
3. **Learn:** VIMO says one line that starts with "So that's why..." or "See?", turning what they saw into the idea.

- No losing. A wrong move gets a funny reaction and a hint, never a "game over".
- Instructions in five words or fewer ("Hold to fire the engine!"), plus a hand icon that shows the move.
- Games teach the idea, not a number. Numbers stay on the card.
- "Spin the 3D model" is never a game on its own: spinning must find something with a reason ("Find the arm that scooped the soil").
- Anything simplified for fun says "for fun, simplified" in small letters.

## Real NASA videos on cards

When NASA has real footage or an official animation that shows the idea better than a photo, the card plays it instead.

- **Short:** trim to the best 5–15 seconds; it loops quietly in the picture area. Tap to play with sound.
- **Tagged:** a small "NASA video" tag (or "NASA animation" for animations), and the credit NASA gives on its page (for example "NASA/JPL-Caltech").
- **Never changed:** only trim, slow down or add captions. No cutting that changes what happened, no AI touch-ups on real footage.
- **Explained:** VIMO says one line over it so a kid knows what they're watching ("This is the real parachute opening over Mars!").
- **Checked:** before release, open NASA's media guidelines and the clip's own page; skip any clip marked as owned by someone else.

## Before building: a quick checklist for every card

- Does it have a picture or a looping clip?
- Can a 10-year-old say what it taught us after reading it?
- Big line 10 words or fewer, "why it matters" 20 words or fewer?
- Does the mini-game end with a "So that's why..." line?
- Exactly one NASA source link?
